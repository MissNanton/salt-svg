The islands make sense differently from above.

Shortly after take-off, the coastline begins to loosen into bays and headlands, volcanic mountains rising behind Saint Vincent as reefs appear beneath the sea and channels separate one island from another, until all thirty-two islands and cays begin to read not as isolated destinations, but as one remarkable archipelago.

SALT Air is being developed as an aerial safari across Saint Vincent & the Grenadines, not simply a scenic flight but a way of understanding the geography of a country most visitors never see whole, let alone from above. The aircraft is only part of the idea.

The Vision

An archipelago, read as one shape

On the ground, Saint Vincent & the Grenadines is experienced sequentially, one island, then another. From the air, those relationships become legible at once: volcanic land gives way to coral islands, reef systems appear beneath the surface, and at Tobago Cays the four-kilometre Horseshoe Reef reveals itself whole, enclosing its cays within a single visible curve of turquoise water.

SALT Air is conceived around that moment of recognition. The flight should provide more than a beautiful view, it should give the guest a new understanding of where they are.

What matters is what becomes visible from it.

The Flight

Ascend. Unfold. Return.

Ascend

Saint Vincent falls away

Guests board a small aircraft near the coast. Within minutes, mountain, town, bay and shoreline begin to appear as one landscape. The country starts changing scale.

Unfold

The islands, one after another

The heart of SALT Air is a carefully considered routing across the Grenadines. From above, reefs, coastlines, channels and settlements reveal the physical logic of the archipelago in a way no map quite manages from the ground.

Interpretation should remain light enough to leave room for looking.

Return

An arrival, not simply a landing

The flight does not need to end the experience. SALT Air may become the opening chapter of a longer private day: landing into Salt & Sorrel, continuing into another island experience, returning for SALT After Dark or forming part of a villa, yacht or hotel itinerary.

The journey from aircraft to what comes next should feel continuous.

The SALT Approach

More than a sightseeing flight.

SALT Air is being developed around three ideas:

  • 01Perspective: The flight should reveal the geography and relationships of the islands rather than simply circle above attractive scenery.
  • 02Interpretation: Guests should leave knowing more about what they saw than when they boarded.
  • 03Hospitality: From briefing to ground transfers and whatever follows the flight, the experience should feel coherent rather than like an aircraft charter with branding added afterwards.

For Aviation & Destination Partners

Built to be operated properly.

SALT does not intend to become an airline. The aviation component of SALT Air must remain in the hands of properly licensed, appropriately equipped aviation professionals. SALT's role is to create the guest experience around that capability: concept, routing ideas, interpretation, hospitality, positioning and integration with hotels, villas, yachts and other SALT experiences.

SALT May Provide

  • Experience concept and guest journey
  • Interpretation and narration design
  • Hospitality and guest briefing
  • Pairing with other SALT experiences
  • Hotel, villa and yacht distribution relationships
  • Brand, editorial and destination storytelling

An Aviation Partner May Provide

  • Appropriately certified aircraft
  • Licensed flight crew
  • Operational control
  • Flight planning and regulatory compliance
  • Safety systems and passenger procedures
  • Aircraft-specific routing and feasibility advice

Hospitality & Destination Partners May Support

  • Guest referrals and concierge sales
  • Ground transfer coordination
  • Departure or arrival access where appropriate
  • Integration with villa, yacht or hotel itineraries
  • Destination and institutional collaboration

Development Status

The idea is ready for the right aircraft.

SALT Air is currently in development. We welcome conversations with aviation operators, hospitality partners and destination stakeholders interested in creating a distinctive aerial experience for Saint Vincent & the Grenadines.

The objective is not simply to put visitors in the sky. It is to create an experience worthy of what they will see from it.
